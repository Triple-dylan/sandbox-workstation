## 2. Agents today, as built

- **A workspace agent per workspace**, created with it (`createProject`). Nothing makes it special afterwards. It can be retired, it has the same page as any other agent, and the only thing it does on its own is keep a hidden Orientation page and answer some unaddressed questions.
- **A private agent per person**, living in the rail and following you across spaces. In your private space it is the only agent, which is right.
- **Specialist agents** (Finance), shared with a workspace or private.
- **Who hears what.** An agent acts only when tagged (`@Finance`), in your rail, or when it's the workspace agent and someone in a channel ends a message with "?" (at most once per cooldown). There is no conversation state: a follow-up without the tag is ignored, even when the agent asked for it.
- **Private to shared.** Your agent can hand work to a workspace's agents (`handoff`); their answer comes back privately to you. Nothing goes the other way: a workspace agent can't consult anyone's private agent, and you can't ask a teammate's private agent anything.
- **People.** Typing `@` offers agents only, never people. Agents can't mention or escalate to a person.
- **Changes.** Agents can propose a new document, an exact text edit to a document, a plan step, a skill or a new agent. A person approves each one. **Sheets, decks and canvases can't be changed by an agent.** Approvals appear as a card where the agent replied, but not when the answer came through a handoff.
- **Seeing agents work.** Orbs show who is working and where. Teammates see your agent working but never what it was asked.

## 3. What people expect: research

Dylan: "you need to do some research on agent interactions for workflows and multiplayer ux principals or somethign. still feel liek some of your suggestions and tests dont necesarily reflect how humns think or where the nomal expectations would be."

What established products and research agree on, and what it means here:

1. **People ask where they are looking.** Excel's Copilot puts an icon next to the cells you select. Its suggestions are about that data, and "Chat with Copilot" opens the side pane with the selection already included. Gemini in Sheets opens beside the sheet and starts by summarizing the data on screen. *Here:* the first place to ask about a budget is the budget. The walkthrough's habit of going to #general to ask was wrong, and so was this plan's first ordering. ([Excel](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/copilot/data-insights-with-copilot-in-excel), [Sheets](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/14356410?hl=en))
2. **Say what it can do and how well, up front.** Microsoft's guidelines for human-AI interaction start with making capabilities and limits clear (G1, G2) and timing help to the context (G3). *Here:* a new agent, and the workspace agent on day one, says in a line what it does and what it can't, instead of a placeholder purpose. ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/))
3. **A conversation with an agent is a thread.** Slack's agent design has an agent reply in a thread and keep the thread as its session, so everything said there reaches it. It shows a working status from the moment it starts, and follows the same visibility rules as people. *Here:* B1 and B2 are the norm, not an extra. ([Slack](https://docs.slack.dev/ai/agent-design/))
4. **Easy to call, dismiss and correct** (Microsoft G7 to G9), and **when unsure, narrow the scope or ask** (G10). Horvitz's mixed-initiative principles add: when unsure, ask in a way that costs little, then let the person refine the result rather than undo it. *Here:* a clarifying question gets a one-tap answer and the same job carries on. A proposed change can be edited before it is accepted, not only accepted or declined. ([Horvitz](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/principles-mixed-initiative-user-interfaces/))
5. **Match the check to the stakes.** Notion lets each agent's approval level be set ("review for me", "always ask"), with a plan mode for big changes. Atlassian warns that reviewing every action breeds review fatigue. *Here:* small, undoable changes (fixing a total, formatting) are made and shown with Undo. Changes to what others rely on are proposed. Multi-step work shows its plan first. The level is set per agent on its page. ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/choose-how-notion-agent-asks-for-your-approval), [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/jira/guides/agentic-engineering/human-in-the-loop))
6. **Group agents speak rarely and in the open.** Teams' Facilitator keeps shared notes in the conversation that update as the group decides, and is called by @mention. Research on agents in group chats ("Time to Talk") separates deciding *whether* to speak from *what* to say, and holds agents to the group's pace. Vendor "listen modes" reply in a thread only when they can help. *Here:* the workspace agent keeps a visible running summary (A4) and only joins a thread when it has something specific. ([Teams](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/facilitator-teams), [Time to Talk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05309v2))
7. **Presence is subtle and controllable.** Figma's multiplayer shows cursors and avatars without names floating over the work; people want presence they can turn down, and they want that remembered. *Here:* agent activity shows as a small mark on the page or row, with a setting to quiet it. ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/multiplayer-editing-in-figma/))
8. **Say who will see it and what will happen** (Microsoft G16, G18). *Here:* every agent answer says where it is visible ("only you", "everyone in #general"). Every change says who it affects before it is made.

What this changes in the plan:
- F (ask on the sheet) moves to the front, and is how agents are tested from now on: on the page, then in a thread, then from the rail.
- C gains approval levels; not every change waits for a yes.
- B is the base for every agent conversation, in channels and on pages.
- D (teammates' private agents) follows the visibility rule: asking Maya's agent is asking Maya, and she decides what her agent shares.

## 4. What the agent model should be

These are proposals, numbered so we can pick. Each one names its user-facing result.

**A. The workspace agent is the workspace's front door** (fixes the "every space needs one" misunderstanding).
1. Exactly one per workspace; it can't be retired. It sits first under Agents, marked as the workspace's own, and its page says what it does: answers anything in the workspace, knows every specialist, and keeps the workspace summary.
2. It introduces itself in #general when the workspace is made, with three concrete first steps (invite people, bring in a file, make a page), and points you to it.
3. It routes. Ask it anything; if a specialist fits (Finance for budget questions), it hands off in the open: "Asking @Finance", with Finance's answer threaded under it. The `handoff` machinery already exists; it just isn't used in channels.
4. Its summary page (today's hidden Orientation) becomes visible and pinned: "What's going on in Northwind Ops", kept current by it.
5. In a private space there is no workspace agent. Your own agent does that job, as now.

**B. Talking to agents in channels works like talking to a person.**
1. Threads: an agent answers in a thread under the message that asked. Anything said in that thread goes to the agent without tagging it. This fixes the "yes" that went nowhere.
2. An agent that asks a question waits for the answer in that thread and continues the same job.
3. `@` offers people and agents together. Agents can tag a person ("@Maya owns travel; asking her") and the person gets it as a mention.

**C. Agents can change what they look after** (fixes the dead-end approval).
1. Sheet changes as proposals: set these cells, add this row. They show as a diff on the sheet with Accept and Decline, like document edits.
2. Every proposal shows wherever the request came from, including your rail after a handoff, and in one "Waiting for you" place. It can be edited before it is accepted.
4. Approval levels per agent, on its page: "Ask me first" (the default for shared agents), "Small fixes without asking" (undoable changes are made and shown with Undo), and "Show the plan first" for multi-step work.
3. A specialist can be given pages to watch ("Finance looks after Q1 Budget"), shown on its page and in the page header. When a watched page crosses a line it has been told ("total above plan"), it says so in the page's feed. That gives proactive help a real trigger, not a "?" at the end of a sentence.

**D. Reaching other people's private agents, with consent** (new).
1. "Ask Maya's agent" from a channel or your rail sends a request card to Maya, not to her agent: "Priya's agent asks: what did the vendor quote for travel?"
2. Maya chooses: her agent drafts an answer from what she can see, she edits or approves it, and only then does it reach Priya. Nothing private leaves without her yes.
3. Teammates' agents show as available or busy, so you know whether asking is worthwhile.

**E. How this shows in the interface.**
1. Agent rows in the list say what each one is doing and what it looks after ("Finance · watching Q1 Budget").
2. A channel's header shows which agents are listening there.
3. The agent page shows: what it looks after, recent asks and their outcomes, who uses it, and how to reach it ("Tag @Finance in any chat, or ask it from your rail").

**F. Ask where you are looking.** The walkthrough asked Finance about the budget from #general, which is not how people work: the question comes up while looking at the sheet.
1. On a sheet, selecting cells offers **Ask** next to them, like selected text in a document does today. The question goes to the agent that looks after the sheet (Finance), with the selected cells as the subject, and the answer appears beside the cells.
2. A page's header shows the agents that look after it, one click to ask them, the same as the rail but already pointed at this page.
3. An answer about specific cells points at them (lit on the sheet), and a suggested change is shown on the cells themselves to accept (C1).

**G. Spreadsheets and connectors: a major piece, not explored yet.** Connectors were in the original scope ("one real connector module" in the first release) and were never built. File import takes CSV and TSV only, not `.xlsx`.
1. **Excel and Google Sheets connectors**: link a workbook or Google Sheet into a workspace as a live sheet page. It stays in sync both ways, with sign-in per person, and agents read and propose changes the same as on a native sheet.
2. **Excel files in and out**: bring in `.xlsx` (every tab), and download a sheet as `.xlsx` or CSV.
3. **Behave like Excel**: copy and paste ranges to and from Excel and Google Sheets (tab-separated, formulas kept where possible), Shift-arrows to select, Ctrl/⌘-arrows to jump, ⌘C/⌘V/⌘X, Delete clears, fill down (⌘D), undo and redo, number formats, column widths, freeze the header row and sorting.
4. **Share tables**: a range shared into a chat stays live (this exists) and can be copied out as a table that pastes cleanly into email, Slack or Excel.
5. Then the same connector pattern for documents (Google Docs, Word on OneDrive) and files (Drive, OneDrive), chosen by what real projects use.

**H. The private chat (your rail).**
1. **Actions fit the answer.** Today every answer offers Share, Use as my reply and Save as a page. A drafted change should offer Apply (as a proposal on the page) and Show me. A plain answer should offer Share and Reply, and Save as a page only when it is document-length. A question back to you should offer quick replies.
2. **Context you can see and steer.** It says what it read ("Q1 Budget, #general, last 30 messages") and you can add or remove a page with a tap. Today it says "reads Whole space" and little more.
3. **Other agents in the open.** When your agent asks Finance, show it as a small exchange you can follow ("asked Finance → Finance answered"), not two separate replies. Let you continue with Finance directly from there.
4. **Remembers the thread.** A follow-up ("make it $3,800 instead") continues the same job and changes the same draft, instead of starting over.
5. **Hand off to people.** "Send this to Maya" drafts a message to her, for you to send.

## 6. Suggested order

1. **F1–F3 with C1–C2**: ask on the sheet, and agents can change it. This is the budget job done where people do it.
2. **A1–A4 and B1–B2**: the workspace agent as the front door, threads and conversation state. This makes agents usable in channels at all.
3. **G2–G3**: Excel files in and out, and the sheet behaves like Excel.
4. **G1**: Excel and Google Sheets connectors.
5. **H1–H5**: the private chat.
6. **B3, E1–E3, C3**: mentions for people, agents showing what they look after, watched pages.
7. **D**: reaching teammates' private agents, with consent.

Each step gets a live check before review, and the evals grow a case for each new agent behaviour (asked-and-answered in a thread, a sheet edit applied, routing to a specialist).
